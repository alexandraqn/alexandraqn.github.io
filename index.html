Job Market Paper

Job Market Paper
The Effect of Public Health Insurance on Labour Supply: Evidence from Indonesia's Askeskin Programme
with Dr. Julia Hatamyar
Public health insurance reshapes economic incentives and household time allocation. This study evaluates the labour market effects of Indonesia's subsidised health insurance programme, Askeskin, introduced in 2005. Using a panel of 13,497 individuals from two waves of the Indonesia Family Life Survey (2000 and 2007), we apply a doubly robust two-period difference-in-differences estimator to adjust for selection into programme enrolment. We find that exposure to Askeskin reduces work at the intensive margin by approximately 1.3 hours per week (p < 0.10), with no significant effect on employment participation or labour income. These reductions are concentrated among women, who work 2.3 hours less per week (p < 0.01), and household heads, who work 2.7 hours less (p < 0.10), consistent with reallocating time toward home production and care-seeking once financial barriers to healthcare fall. Among baseline informal workers, Askeskin increases transitions into formal employment by 2.5 percentage points (p < 0.01) and reduces remaining informal by 2.9 percentage points (p < 0.10), acting as a subsidy to formalisation rather than a deterrent. Overall, public health insurance reduces precautionary labour supply and reshapes employment composition among workers most vulnerable to health-related income shocks.

Working Papers & Research in Progress

The Impact of Expanding Child Health Insurance on Parental Labour Supply: Evidence from Mexico’s Seguro Popular Universal Health Insurance
Single-authored Working Paper
Public health insurance programmes in low- and middle-income countries aim to protect families from catastrophic medical expenses, yet their effects on parental time allocation remain ambiguous. This paper examines whether age-based eligibility thresholds in public child health coverage in Mexico affect parental employment and working hours. Applying a sharp regression discontinuity design at the 60-month child-age cut-off across 447,355 observations from the Mexican Labour Force Survey (2007–2008), I find tightly estimated null effects on overall parental labour supply, ruling out impacts larger than 0.8 weekly hours or 1.4 percentage points in employment at the 95% confidence level. Exploratory heterogeneity analysis points to a modest half-hour weekly increase among mothers (p < 0.10), suggesting that child health coverage may slightly ease maternal caregiving constraints.

Hospitalisations and Within-Household Labour Reallocation: Evidence of Family Spillovers in Indonesia
with Dr. Julia Hatamyar and Prof. Luigi Siciliani
Examines intra-household labour supply spillovers following family hospitalisation shocks using microdata from the 2019 Indonesian Socio-Economic Survey (SUSENAS), estimated via Augmented Inverse Probability Weighting (AIPW) and Coarsened Exact Matching (CEM). We uncover stark gender asymmetries: residing with a hospitalised relative reduces a woman's employment probability by 2.0 to 2.8 percentage points and decreases weekly labour supply by 1.29 hours. Conversely, male labour supply is unaffected by family hospitalisations, showing that domestic caregiving burdens fall disproportionately on women.
