Job Market Paper

Job Market Paper · Single-Authored
The Impact of Expanding Child Health Insurance on Parental Labour Supply: Evidence from Mexico’s Seguro Popular Universal Health Insurance
Single-authored Working Paper
Universal health insurance reforms in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) are designed to mitigate catastrophic health expenditures, yet they also alter household economic constraints, precautionary labour supply, and domestic caregiving burdens. This paper evaluates the labour supply impacts of expanding public health insurance coverage to children under five in Mexico through Seguro Popular. Using a sharp Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD) at the five-year age eligibility cutoff across 447,355 individual-quarter observations from the Mexican National Employment Survey (ENOE 2007–2008), I estimate the Local Average Treatment Effect (LATE) of eligibility under flexible bandwidth specifications. While I find no aggregate effect on overall parental labour force participation, significant gendered heterogeneities emerge. Mothers of children near the eligibility cutoff increase their labour supply intensity by an average of 2 hours per month (p < 0.10). These findings indicate that expanding child health coverage generates intra-household spillovers by alleviating maternal caregiving burdens and unlocking secondary labour supply.

Working Papers & Research in Progress

The Effect of Public Health Insurance on Labour Supply: Evidence from Indonesia's Askeskin Programme
with Dr. Julia Hatamyar
Evaluates the labour market impacts of Indonesia’s subsidised health insurance programme (Askeskin) using panel microdata from the Indonesia Family Life Survey (IFLS 2000 & 2007, N = 13,497). Applying a doubly robust difference-in-differences estimator, we find that health coverage reduces weekly labour supply by 1.3 hours (p < 0.10), concentrated among women (-2.3 hrs) and household heads. Crucially, Askeskin increases the probability of informal workers transitioning into formal employment by 2.5 percentage points (p < 0.01), demonstrating that health insurance reduces precautionary labour supply while facilitating labour market formalisation.
📄 Presented at EEA Dublin 2026, EuHEA Rotterdam 2026, HESG Glasgow 2026.
Hospitalisations and Within-Household Labour Reallocation: Evidence of Family Spillovers in Indonesia
with Dr. Julia Hatamyar and Prof. Luigi Siciliani
Examines intra-household labour supply spillovers following family hospitalisation shocks using microdata from the 2019 Indonesian Socio-Economic Survey (SUSENAS), estimated via Augmented Inverse Probability Weighting (AIPW) and Coarsened Exact Matching (CEM). We uncover stark gender asymmetries: residing with a hospitalised relative reduces a woman's employment probability by 2.0 to 2.8 percentage points and decreases weekly labour supply by 1.29 hours. Conversely, male labour supply is unaffected by family hospitalisations, showing that domestic caregiving burdens fall disproportionately on women.
📄 To be Presented at HESG London 2027.
The Relationship Between Insulin Prices and the COMISCA Joint Negotiation Mechanism
with Prof. Rodrigo Moreno-Serra, M. Campos, P. Lagos, and M. García Gonzales
Applies an event-study empirical framework on pharmaceutical procurement microdata across 5 Central American countries to evaluate the pricing impacts of regional joint negotiation mechanisms for essential diabetes medication.
